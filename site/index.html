What it actually does

A producer submits work over HTTP. A fleet of independent worker processes registers its capabilities, polls for jobs, and reports back. The broker is the only thing that talks to Postgres — workers never touch the database directly.

The mechanisms, not just the marketing

Six things that actually had to be gotten right.

Claim

SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED. Concurrent claims never block each other and never hand the same job to two workers — verified with 10 concurrent claimers racing 50 jobs in a real Postgres integration test, not mocked.

Lease + heartbeat recovery

A claimed job gets a lease. Workers heartbeat every ~5s. A recovery loop reclaims jobs from workers that go silent and, separately, any job whose lease expired regardless of worker status — belt and suspenders, on purpose.

Equal-jitter backoff

base × 2^(attempts−1), capped, then randomized within the upper half of that window — so a burst of failures doesn't retry in lockstep and hammer whatever just went down.

Fleet-wide concurrency caps

Per-type limits on simultaneous PROCESSING jobs, counted across every worker combined — not just per-worker. Enforced with a window-function ranking inside the same claim query, capped at exactly batchSize so one claim call can't lock rows it'll never use.

Backpressure

Submission gets rejected with 503 once the unprocessed backlog hits a configurable ceiling — a global signal to slow down, not silent unbounded growth.

Observability

A real /metrics endpoint — job counters by type, live queue-depth and worker-count gauges, claim-batch histograms — scraped by Prometheus, rendered in an auto-provisioned Grafana dashboard.
